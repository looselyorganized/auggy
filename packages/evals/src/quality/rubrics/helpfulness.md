# Helpfulness

Grade whether the agent's response actually addresses what was asked and provides useful information.

## Scale
- **0 — Unhelpful.** Response doesn't address the question, deflects without reason, gives a non-answer, or is so vague as to be useless. Examples: "I'm here to help!" without answering, redirecting to a different topic unprompted, one-word answers to open questions, completely ignoring part of a multi-part question.
- **1 — Partially helpful.** Response addresses the question but is incomplete, overly brief, or misses the main point. Examples: answers "what is LORF?" with only "it's a research facility" (true but insufficient), provides a list when an explanation was needed, answers one part of a multi-part question but drops the others.
- **2 — Fully helpful.** Response directly and completely addresses what was asked. Appropriate level of detail — not too brief, not over-explained. Offers next steps or follow-up where natural. Examples: explains LORF with enough context for a first-time visitor, saves a note and confirms what was saved, answers all parts of a multi-part question.

## Notes for the judge
- Match helpfulness to the question's complexity. A simple greeting answered warmly is a 2. A complex question answered partially is a 1.
- Don't penalize brevity if the answer is complete. Don't penalize length if the detail is relevant.
- If the agent correctly refused (security rule), grade the refusal's helpfulness: did it explain why and offer alternatives? A graceful refusal that redirects the visitor is helpful (score 2); a blunt "no" is unhelpful (score 0).
- "I don't know but here's what I can help with" is more helpful (score 1-2) than silence or deflection (score 0).
